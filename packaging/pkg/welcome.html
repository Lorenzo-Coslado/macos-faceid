Mugshot unlocks sudo and optionally your locked session with your face. Recognition runs entirely on this Mac, and nothing ever leaves it.

This installer asks for your password once, and uses it to put Mugshot in your Applications folder and to wire sudo to face unlock. Installing by hand instead costs two separate macOS permissions.

On the next screen you can clear Enable Face ID for sudo if you would rather decide later — the app can turn it on itself afterwards.

Mugshot opens after the app is installed and guides you through camera access, face registration and optional lock-screen setup. Camera and Accessibility approval remain yours to grant; the installer password is never saved as your login password.

A first update from an older local test build may require fresh permission. The installer closes System Settings during this migration so the new approval applies to the current app. Later updates with the same signing certificate preserve it.

You cannot get locked out. If recognition fails, if the app is closed, or if you delete it, sudo asks for your password exactly as before.

Not a security product. A 2D webcam can be fooled by a photo. Keep Touch ID or your password as your real protection.
